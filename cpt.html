What the exam is

The NHA Certified Phlebotomy Technician exam has 120 questions: 100 are scored and 20 are unscored pretest items. You get 2 hours. That is one minute per question with no slack, so the exam rewards knowing the material cold rather than reasoning each item out from scratch.

A new version of the exam went live on January 7, 2026. It comes from a job analysis NHA completed in 2024, and NHA's own announcement described the change as having minimal impact on topics and content areas. Study materials written for the previous test plan are not wrong, but they were not built to its weights.

The test plan groups everything into five domains: Safety and Compliance, Patient Preparation, Routine Blood Collections, Special Collections, and Processing. It also lists a block of Core Knowledge (anatomy of the circulatory system, medical terminology, and the like) that is not a domain of its own: those facts show up inside whichever domain applies them.

How it is scored

NHA does not grade the CPT as a percentage. Every NHA exam is reported on a scaled score, and the passing score is 390 out of 500. NHA publishes that number in its annual pass-rate report, and it does not publish how many correct answers map to 390, because that depends on the difficulty of the particular set of questions you get.

For the same reason, this app cannot tell you "you would have passed". What it can do is hold you to a study target: the practice tests here use 70 % as the bar, and the guide says so on every result screen. If you clear 70 % consistently on tests that follow the official split, you are studying at the level the exam expects.

In 2024, NHA administered 57,457 CPT exams and 75.96 % of candidates passed. Roughly one in four did not. The domains where that quarter loses points are the ones with a single right answer: order of draw, tube additives, tourniquet time, needle angle.

Source: NHA, Pass Rates for NHA Examinations Administered in 2024 (revised 1/8/25); NHA CPT Test Plan

What the questions look like

NHA’s published CPT test plan describes the domains and the number of questions, and does not describe the item formats. We do not describe a format the certifying body has not published.

What it costs to sit

The National Healthcareer Association (NHA) sets and publishes the exam fee, and it is not stated in the outline documents this page is built from. Check the current fee with the certifying body before you apply.

The exam changed in January 2026

NHA released updated CPT study materials on October 8, 2025, and the new certification exam on January 7, 2026. Anyone testing after that date sits the new version; there is no choice between old and new.

The five domains and their weights in this app are the ones in the 2024-job-analysis test plan, which is the plan for the January 2026 exam. If you are using a book or a course written before 2025, compare its table of contents with the areas listed here; the names and the emphasis are what changed.

Source: NHA News Center, New CPT Certification Exam & Study Materials Available; NHA CPT Test Plan

Keeping the credential

Passing is the start of a cycle, not the end of one. This is what the National Healthcareer Association (NHA) requires afterwards.

  • Recertify every two years.
  • Ten continuing education credits per cycle, where two hours of continuing education equal one NHA credit. Leftover credits do not carry over to the next cycle.
  • There is a one-month grace period; not meeting the requirement before the expiration date voids the credential.
  • Reinstatement is possible within one year of expiry with 15 CE credits, plus the recertification and reinstatement fees. After one year there is no reinstatement: you re-register, retake the exam and pay the full price.
  • A provisional certification expires twelve months after the exam and can be neither recertified nor reinstated.
  • NHA’s own blog gives the renewal fee as $195, and $65 for each additional NHA certification with no extra continuing education. That figure comes from the blog, not from the fee page, which we could not open.

Source: NHA Candidate Handbook; NHA Help Center, How do I keep my NHA certification current; NHA blog, How do I renew my NHA certification.

How to study for it

  • Work in the proportions above. A practice test that pulls at random from a whole question bank hides your weak areas by burying them among the strong ones; one built with the published split shows you where the exam will actually hurt.
  • Train at the pace of the real thing: this exam runs at about 1 minute per question.
  • Study by area, not by volume. Finish one area, then measure it, then move on. A score of 90 % on eight questions is not mastery of anything.
  • Read the explanation of the questions you got right, not only of the ones you missed. A lucky guess and a correct answer look identical on a score line.
  • Answer every question as if it counts: you cannot tell the unscored pretest items apart from the rest.
